# 🔎 Pronouns — Examples

> Ya conocemos los diferentes tipos de pronouns. Ahora vamos a verlos trabajando juntos dentro de oraciones reales.

---

## 🎯 Objetivo

Al terminar este tema podrás:

- reconocer los diferentes pronouns dentro de una oración;
- identificar la función que cumple cada uno;
- entender por qué cambia `I → me → my → mine → myself`;
- analizar oraciones completas;
- empezar a utilizar los pronouns de forma más natural.

---

# 🧠 Nuestro mapa

Antes de comenzar, recordemos nuestra tabla principal:

| Subject | Object | Possessive Adjective | Possessive Pronoun | Reflexive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | me | my | mine | myself |
| you | you | your | yours | yourself / yourselves |
| he | him | his | his | himself |
| she | her | her | hers | herself |
| it | it | its | — | itself |
| we | us | our | ours | ourselves |
| they | them | their | theirs | themselves |

No queremos memorizar solamente esta tabla.

Queremos reconocer:

```text
Who does the action?
↓
Subject Pronoun

Who receives the action?
↓
Object Pronoun

Whose + noun?
↓
Possessive Adjective

Whose without repeating the noun?
↓
Possessive Pronoun

Does it refer back to the Subject?
↓
Reflexive Pronoun
```

# 1️⃣ Ejemplos con I

Observa esta familia:

```text
I → me → my → mine → myself
```

### Subject Pronoun

> **I** work as a software engineer.

`I` realiza la acción.

---

### Object Pronoun

> Maria called **me**.

`me` recibe la acción.

---

### Possessive Adjective

> This is **my** computer.

Tenemos:

```text
my + computer
```

---

### Possessive Pronoun

> This computer is **mine**.

`mine` reemplaza:

> my computer

---

### Reflexive Pronoun

> I built the application **myself**.

`myself` enfatiza que yo mismo la construí.

---

# 2️⃣ Ejemplos con You

Nuestra familia:

```text
you → you → your → yours → yourself / yourselves
```

### Subject

> **You** work here.

### Object

> I called **you**.

### Possessive Adjective

> This is **your** laptop.

### Possessive Pronoun

> This laptop is **yours**.

### Reflexive

Una persona:

> You should believe in **yourself**.

Varias personas:

> You should introduce **yourselves**.

---

# 3️⃣ Ejemplos con He

Nuestra familia:

```text
he → him → his → his → himself
```

### Subject

> **He** works from home.

### Object

> I work with **him**.

### Possessive Adjective

> This is **his** computer.

### Possessive Pronoun

> This computer is **his**.

### Reflexive

> He fixed the computer **himself**.

Observa que `his` no cambia:

```text
his computer
↓
Possessive Adjective

The computer is his.
↓
Possessive Pronoun
```

# 4️⃣ Ejemplos con She

Nuestra familia:

```text
she → her → her → hers → herself
```

### Subject

> **She** works with me.

### Object

> I work with **her**.

### Possessive Adjective

> This is **her** desk.

### Possessive Pronoun

> This desk is **hers**.

### Reflexive

> She designed the website **herself**.

Aquí tenemos algo importante:

```text
her
```

> Object Pronoun

> Possessive Adjective

# 🔎 Ahora vamos a mezclarlos

Hasta aquí los vimos por familias.

# 🧩 Example 1

> **She** called **me** yesterday.

Analizamos:

```text
She
↓
Subject Pronoun

called
↓
Verb

me
↓
Object Pronoun
```

La estructura principal es:

```text
Subject + Verb + Object
```

---

# 🧩 Example 2

> **I** showed **her** my computer.

Tenemos:

```text
I
↓
Subject Pronoun

her
↓
Object Pronoun

my
↓
Possessive Adjective

computer
↓
Noun
```

Observa que `her` no significa posesión aquí.

¿Por qué?

Porque:

> I showed **her**...

`her` es la persona relacionada con la acción de `showed`.

En cambio:

> **my computer**

sí expresa posesión.

---

> **She** showed **me** her computer.

Ahora tenemos:

```text

Subject Pronoun

↓
Object Pronoun

her
↓
Possessive Adjective

computer
↓
Noun
```

Observa:

```text
her computer
```

Aquí `her` es Possessive Adjective porque tenemos un noun después.

# 🧩 Example 9

> **He** saw **himself** in the mirror.

Tenemos:

```text
He
↓
Subject Pronoun

↓
Reflexive Pronoun
```

Aquí no es solamente énfasis.

```text
He
↓

↓
himself
```

`He` y `himself` representan a la misma persona.

# 🧩 Example 10

> **I** saw **him** in the office.

Ahora:

```text
I
↓
Subject

him
↓
Object
```

Tenemos dos personas diferentes:

```text
I ≠ him
```

Por eso utilizamos:

> him

y no:

> himself

---

# 🔄 Him vs Himself

Comparemos directamente.

> She called **him**.

```text
She ≠ him
```

Pero:

> He called **himself**.

```text
He = himself
```

La pregunta importante es:

> ¿El pronoun se refiere al mismo Subject?

---

# 🔄 Her vs Herself

> Carlos saw **her**.

```text
Carlos ≠ her
```

Pero:

> She saw **herself** in the mirror.

```text
She = herself
```

---

# 🧩 Example 11 — Her puede cambiar de función

Observa:

> **She** gave **her** her book.

Puede parecer extraño, pero es gramaticalmente posible.

Analicemos:

```text
She
↓
Subject Pronoun

her
↓
Object Pronoun

her
↓
Possessive Adjective

book
↓
Noun
```

Tenemos dos `her`.

Pero no cumplen la misma función.

Podemos imaginar:

> Maria gave Laura Maria's book.

Dependiendo del contexto:

> She gave her her book.

Esto demuestra por qué debemos entender la **función**, no solamente memorizar palabras.

---

> I showed him mine.

# 🧩 Example 13 — Your vs Yours

> Is this **your** laptop?

Aquí:

```text
your + laptop
↓
Possessive Adjective
```

Pero podemos preguntar:

> Is this laptop **yours**?

Aquí:

```text
yours
↓
Possessive Pronoun
```

Ambas preguntas hablan de posesión.

La estructura cambia.

# 🧠 El objetivo real

Cuando leas:

> They showed us their project.

No queremos que tu mente haga esto:

```text
They = ellos
us = nosotros
their = su
```

Queremos llegar progresivamente a:

```text
They
↓
Subject

us
↓
Object

their project
↓
Possession
```

Es decir:

> **entender la función directamente en inglés.**

La traducción puede ayudarnos al principio, pero no debe ser siempre necesaria.

# 🔍 Método de análisis

Cuando encuentres un pronoun, puedes preguntarte:

### 1. ¿Realiza la acción?

```text
Subject Pronoun
```

### 2. ¿Recibe la acción?

```text
Object Pronoun
```

### 3. ¿Expresa posesión y tiene un noun?

```text
Possessive Adjective
```

### 4. ¿Expresa posesión y reemplaza al noun?

```text
Possessive Pronoun
```

### 5. ¿Se refiere nuevamente al mismo Subject?

```text
Reflexive Pronoun
```

# 📝 Resumen

Ya podemos reconocer las cinco familias:

```text
Subject Pronouns
↓
I / you / he / she / it / we / they

Object Pronouns
↓
me / you / him / her / it / us / them

Possessive Adjectives
↓
my / your / his / her / its / our / their

Possessive Pronouns
↓
mine / yours / his / hers / ours / theirs

Reflexive Pronouns
↓
myself / yourself / himself / herself
itself / ourselves / yourselves / themselves
```

Pero lo más importante no es memorizar los nombres.

Es entender:

```text
I called her.
↑        ↑
Subject  Object

This is her computer.
        ↑
        Possession + Noun

The computer is hers.
                ↑
                Possession without Noun

She fixed it herself.
             ↑
             Same person / Emphasis
```

---

# ✅ Debes recordar

1. Un mismo referente puede necesitar diferentes pronouns según su función.
2. `I → me → my → mine → myself`.
3. `he → him → his → his → himself`.
4. `she → her → her → hers → herself`.
5. `we → us → our → ours → ourselves`.
6. `they → them → their → theirs → themselves`.
7. Un Possessive Adjective acompaña a un noun.
8. Un Possessive Pronoun reemplaza al noun.
9. Un Reflexive Pronoun puede indicar que la acción vuelve al Subject o añadir énfasis.
10. El objetivo es reconocer la función sin depender siempre de la traducción.
